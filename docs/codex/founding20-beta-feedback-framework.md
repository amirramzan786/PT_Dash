# Project Steel — Founding 20 Beta Feedback Framework

**Status:** Authoritative beta research framework for the Founding 20 programme.

This document defines when Project Steel asks Founding 20 members for feedback, the exact questions to use, how feedback is classified, and how the team should interpret the results. The aim is to capture useful product evidence without pestering users or turning every comment into a feature request.

## 1. Core principle

Founders should be rewarded and respected, not treated like unpaid QA staff.

Feedback should be:
- lightweight;
- triggered by meaningful use rather than arbitrary pop-ups;
- easy to skip;
- collected primarily in-app;
- tied to real product behaviour where practical;
- never required to retain Founding status or lifetime Premium.

## 2. Feedback cadence

### Stage A — First impression

**Trigger:** onboarding/account setup completed and the user has reached the main app experience.

Do not interrupt immediately. Show the pulse after the user has had enough time to look around or on the next natural return to Settings/Support.

Questions:
1. **What was your first impression of Steel?**
   - Very clear
   - Mostly clear
   - Mixed
   - Confusing
2. **Was there anything you expected to see but could not find?**
   - Optional free text
3. **What made you want to try Steel?**
   - Training in one place
   - Nutrition in one place
   - Progress tracking
   - Recovery / whole-picture view
   - Founding 20 lifetime Premium
   - Curiosity / supporting the project
   - Other

Purpose: measure expectation, onboarding clarity and initial value proposition.

### Stage B — After three completed workouts

**Trigger:** after the third successfully saved workout session.

Questions:
1. **How easy was it to use Steel during your workouts?**
   - Very easy
   - Easy
   - Okay
   - Difficult
   - Very difficult
2. **What slowed you down or felt annoying?**
   - Optional free text
3. **What part of the workout experience do you like most?**
   - Workout plan
   - Exercise guidance
   - Logging sets/reps/weight
   - Session flow
   - Progress/history
   - Other
4. **Would you use Steel for your next workout without being reminded?**
   - Yes
   - Probably
   - Unsure
   - Probably not
   - No

Purpose: assess real training usability and early habit potential.

### Stage C — Day 7 value check

**Trigger:** around seven days after verified account activation, provided the user has used the app meaningfully.

Questions:
1. **Which parts of Steel have you actually used this week?**
   - Training
   - Nutrition / meal plan
   - Weight
   - Progress
   - Weekly check-in
   - Exercise library
   - Other
2. **Which part feels most valuable to you right now?**
   - Single-select from the same list
3. **Which part have you ignored or not understood?**
   - Optional free text
4. **What is the one thing you would improve first?**
   - Optional free text
5. **Would you recommend Steel to someone who trains?**
   - Definitely
   - Probably
   - Unsure
   - Probably not
   - No

Purpose: identify value concentration, dead features and first-week friction.

### Stage D — Day 14 retention / product-value check

**Trigger:** approximately 14 days after activation for members who have returned at least once after their first day.

Primary question:
**How would you feel if you could no longer use Steel tomorrow?**
- Very disappointed
- Somewhat disappointed
- Not disappointed
- I have not used it enough to know

Follow-up:
**What is the main reason for your answer?**
- Free text

Additional questions:
- **What do you currently use Steel instead of, or alongside?**
- **What would make Steel worth paying for if you were not a Founding member?**
- **What would stop you using Steel long term?**

Purpose: early product-market-fit and willingness-to-pay evidence.

### Stage E — Founder beta review

**Trigger:** end of the structured Founding 20 beta period, ideally after 3–4 weeks of opportunity to use the product.

Questions:
1. **How often did you use Steel during the beta?**
   - Most training days
   - Weekly
   - A few times
   - Once or twice
2. **What would you genuinely miss if Steel disappeared?**
3. **What should Steel remove, simplify or stop doing?**
4. **What should Steel improve before a public launch?**
5. **Compared with how you tracked fitness before, Steel is:**
   - Much better
   - Better
   - About the same
   - Worse
   - Much worse
6. **If you were not a Founding member, which price would feel reasonable for the current product?**
   - £0 / I would not pay
   - Up to £2.99/month
   - £3–£4.99/month
   - £5–£7.99/month
   - £8–£11.99/month
   - £12+/month
7. **Would you personally recommend Steel today?**
   - Yes
   - Maybe
   - No
8. **Would you be happy for us to contact you about using a short quote from your feedback as a testimonial?**
   - Yes
   - No

Important: a Yes to testimonial contact is permission to ask, not permission to publish a quote. Obtain explicit approval of the exact quote before public use.

## 3. Always-available in-app feedback

Settings > Support should contain **Beta Feedback** at all times.

Categories:
- Bug / something broke
- Confusing / hard to use
- Feature request
- Training
- Nutrition
- Progress / recovery
- Other

Primary prompt:
**What happened — or what would you change?**

Optional fields:
- What were you trying to do?
- How important is this to you?
  - Low
  - Medium
  - High
  - Blocking

Success message:
**Feedback received. Thank you for helping build Steel.**

## 4. Feedback classification

Every item should be assigned one primary type:

### BUG
Something does not work as designed.

Examples:
- save action fails;
- wrong data shown;
- UI element blocks interaction;
- crash/error.

### FRICTION
The feature technically works but is confusing, slow, awkward or hard to discover.

Examples:
- too many taps during a workout;
- unclear wording;
- user cannot find an existing feature;
- mobile layout slows logging.

### FEATURE REQUEST
The user wants functionality that does not currently exist.

Do not treat a request as automatically important. Record the underlying job/problem as well as the requested solution.

### VALUE SIGNAL
Evidence that Steel solves a meaningful problem or becomes part of the user's routine.

Examples:
- user replaces another app;
- user says a specific Steel capability is hard to give up;
- user repeatedly uses a feature without prompting;
- user would recommend/pay for the product.

## 5. Priority scoring

Use a simple evidence score rather than reacting to the loudest user.

For bugs/friction:
- Severity: 1–4
- Frequency: 1–4
- Core-flow impact: 0–2

**Priority score = Severity + Frequency + Core-flow impact**

Working interpretation:
- 8–10: fix before expanding beta
- 6–7: high priority
- 4–5: normal backlog
- 1–3: monitor

For feature requests, assess:
- number of independent users asking;
- whether it supports Steel's core Train → Fuel → Recover → Progress proposition;
- whether users are already trying to solve the problem elsewhere;
- implementation cost/complexity;
- whether it improves acquisition, activation, retention or willingness to pay.

One founder request alone is not a roadmap commitment.

## 6. Commercial signals to watch

The beta should produce evidence around:
- verified signup conversion;
- account activation;
- first workout completion;
- 3-workout completion;
- Day 7 return;
- Day 14 return;
- breadth of feature use;
- repeated use of core features;
- recommendation intent;
- price willingness;
- disappointment if Steel disappeared.

A particularly useful PMF-style signal is the percentage answering **Very disappointed** to the Day 14 question. Do not use one small Founding 20 cohort as definitive statistical proof, but treat the result as directional evidence.

## 7. Warm vs independent feedback

Tag recruitment source so we can compare:
- warm / founder network;
- independent / cold recruitment.

Do not assume warm users are invalid, but give extra weight to behaviour and feedback from users with no personal relationship to the founder when assessing commercial demand.

## 8. UK vs international

Record country at a coarse country level only when legitimately available/collected for beta analysis.

Compare UK and international cohorts for:
- signup conversion;
- activation;
- feature usage;
- retention;
- value proposition;
- willingness to pay.

Do not over-interpret very small geographical samples.

## 9. Founder recognition

Founding members should see their Founder status and lifetime Premium benefit inside Settings once the Founding entitlement system is live.

The feedback programme must never threaten or condition that benefit. Feedback is encouraged, not contractually required.

Future physical merchandise is intentionally **not part of the beta promise**. It may be considered later once Steel generates sufficient cash and unit economics justify it.

## 10. Notification rules

Do not bombard users.

Rules:
- never show more than one structured feedback prompt in a single app session;
- allow `Not now` / dismiss;
- do not repeatedly show a dismissed prompt that day;
- do not interrupt an active workout;
- do not interrupt account/security actions;
- prefer post-action or Settings/Home moments;
- always keep manual Beta Feedback available.

## 11. Internal beta review rhythm

During the first 20:

### Twice weekly
Review:
- new bugs;
- blocking friction;
- repeated feedback themes;
- activation/retention anomalies.

### Weekly
Summarise:
- top 3 problems;
- top 3 value signals;
- features being ignored;
- behaviour vs stated feedback;
- warm vs cold difference;
- UK vs international difference where sample permits.

Do not redesign the product every day. Batch non-critical changes to preserve a stable enough product for useful testing.

## 12. Beta exit decision

The Founding 20 programme is not complete merely because 20 emails have been allocated.

Before expanding to 25–50 users, we should have:
- enough activated users to judge real usage;
- no unresolved critical signup/auth/data-loss issues;
- workout logging usable without major blockers;
- evidence that at least one core Steel value proposition is repeatedly used;
- meaningful Day 7/Day 14 return behaviour;
- qualitative feedback from both warm and independent users;
- initial willingness-to-pay evidence;
- clear list of what must be fixed before a wider cohort.

The subsequent Commercial Gate document should convert those signals into explicit GO / ITERATE / STOP thresholds.
