# Project Steel — Founding 20 Recruitment Plan

**Status:** Working recruitment plan for the first 20 verified beta members.
**Purpose:** Recruit a deliberately mixed cohort that gives both fast product feedback and credible commercial validation.

## Cohort structure

Target: **20 total verified beta members**.

- **10 warm recruits** sourced personally by the founder.
- **10 cold / independent recruits** who do not know the founder personally.

The first 20 verified users are still allocated strictly by the live Founding 20 verification rules. This recruitment plan does not manually reserve a founder number.

## Warm cohort — current position

Current identified group:
- 5 mates who are gym-goers, mostly intermediate level and above.
- 4 are rated **A-tier**: expected to use Steel properly and give useful feedback.
- 1 is rated **B-tier**: likely useful, but not relied on for the highest-value feedback.

Warm recruitment remaining: **5 people**.

Preferred profiles for the remaining warm recruits:
1. 2 beginners / people returning to training.
2. 1 nutrition- or macro-focused user.
3. 1 heavy user of an existing fitness app such as Hevy, Strong, MyFitnessPal, MacroFactor or similar.
4. 1 less-technical user / wildcard who can expose usability problems.

Avoid filling all remaining warm places with more experienced lifters; that profile is already represented.

## Cold cohort

Target: **10 independent testers**.

Preferred geographic split:
- roughly 5 UK users;
- roughly 5 international users.

For the international half, prioritise English-speaking markets initially to avoid confusing product validation with localisation issues. Useful starting markets include the US, Canada, Australia, Ireland and New Zealand.

Preferred tester mix across the cold cohort:
- intermediate / advanced gym users;
- beginners or returners;
- nutrition / macro-focused users;
- users already relying on competing fitness apps;
- at least one wildcard profile.

The exact mix can flex to fill gaps left by the warm cohort.

## Tester qualification rule

A strong Founding 20 candidate should meet at least **3 of these 5**:

1. Trains, or genuinely intends to train, consistently — ideally 2+ times per week.
2. Is willing to use Steel for at least 2–4 weeks rather than opening it once.
3. Will log real workouts, nutrition or progress rather than only browsing the app.
4. Is willing to give direct feedback about what is useful, confusing, missing or pointless.
5. Is comfortable being contacted for short beta-feedback follow-ups.

Avoid prioritising people who are joining only for the lifetime-free offer, are unlikely to train during the beta, or are unlikely to provide meaningful usage/feedback.

## Recruitment channels

Primary channels agreed:
- Facebook
- Instagram
- Snapchat
- Reddit

The same core proposition should be maintained across platforms, but the tone should be native to each channel.

### Facebook
Longer, conversational post suitable for personal feed, groups and communities. Explain what Steel is, why testers are needed, what is expected from them and the Founding 20 offer.

### Instagram
Visual-first feed post plus Stories. Keep the copy shorter and stronger, with a clear CTA to request beta access.

### Snapchat
Very short, direct Story/Snap messaging. Personal tone, strong Founding 20 hook, direct link or reply CTA.

### Reddit
Least promotional tone. Lead with the problem: training, nutrition, recovery and progress being split across multiple apps. Ask for a small number of genuine testers and explicitly request honest criticism. Follow each subreddit/community's self-promotion rules before posting.

## Core public proposition

Use this as the consistent message underneath platform-specific copy:

> Project Steel is a fitness platform bringing training, nutrition, recovery and progress into one place. We are opening a small beta to people who will actually use it and give proper feedback. The first 20 verified beta members get core Steel Premium free for the lifetime of their account. No card required.

Do not market Steel as revolutionary or overstate unvalidated claims. The beta should attract people because the actual product problem and proposition appeal to them.

## Warm outreach principle

Warm outreach should be personal rather than corporate. The ask is not just "try my app"; it is to use Steel properly for a few weeks and tell us what works, what is annoying and what is missing.

## Cold outreach principle

Cold recruitment is a commercial-validation test. A stranger who:
1. discovers Steel,
2. requests access,
3. verifies,
4. activates,
5. uses it,
6. returns,

provides much stronger evidence of product appeal than someone participating mainly as a favour.

## What we want to learn from the first 20

The first cohort should answer:
- Does the all-in-one training / nutrition / recovery / progress proposition make sense immediately?
- Can users start using Steel without hand-holding?
- Which feature becomes the main reason they return?
- Where does the app create friction or confusion?
- Do users replace or reduce use of another app?
- Do users return during week one and weeks two to four?
- Would non-founder users recommend Steel to another person?
- After using it, would a non-Founding user plausibly pay for Steel?

## Success discipline

Do not judge the beta by compliments or number of signups alone. Judge it by verified signups, activation, real product activity, retention and useful feedback.

The analytics plan in `docs/codex/founding20-analytics-plan.md` remains the source of truth for funnel measurement.

## Recruitment sequence

1. Finish production signup/verification system and QA it.
2. Confirm legal/privacy/beta copy reflects the real implemented flow.
3. Invite the strongest warm testers first.
4. Begin social recruitment across Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat and Reddit.
5. Track recruitment source separately from user permissions/entitlements.
6. Stop advertising the lifetime offer automatically once 20 valid Founders have been allocated; continue waitlist recruitment if desired.
7. Review activation and early feedback before deliberately expanding toward the 25–50-user beta.
