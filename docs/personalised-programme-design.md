# Personalised programme foundation

Project Steel will generate programmes from a structured coaching intake, not by ranking a fixed set of body-part workout names.

## Intake signals

The intake is progressive and each sensitive field is optional. It captures:

- primary goal and target timeframe;
- experience, recent training frequency and current activity;
- weekly training days, preferred session duration, location and available equipment;
- exercise likes, exercises to avoid, limitations and recovery/sleep context;
- preferred training style, cardio preference and cardio experience;
- dietary preference, allergies, meal frequency, cooking practicalities and preferred foods.

## Plan rules

The generator chooses a template before it chooses exercises:

| Situation | Starting programme direction |
| --- | --- |
| Beginner or 1–3 days | Full body, conservative volume and simple movement patterns |
| 4 days with gym access | Upper/lower or a goal-appropriate split |
| 5–6 days with adequate experience | PPL, strength/hypertrophy block or conditioning blend |
| Fitness or fat-loss goal | Strength base plus cardio matched to current capacity and preference |
| Strength goal | Lower repetition ranges, longer rests, controlled progression |
| Limitation/recovery concern | Conservative exercise substitutions, lower volume and no unsafe progression |

Each generated version records the selected split, exercises, sets, reps, RPE/intensity, starting-load method, rest periods, cardio prescription and progression rule. The app must explain the reason for each recommendation and avoid unsafe volume spikes.

## Ownership and changes

- Free users can adjust preferences and make session-level swaps.
- AI subscribers receive limited full-plan refreshes.
- PT-managed plans are locked: clients can log alternatives or request a change, while the trainer approves programme-level edits.
- Every programme update creates a version with author, timestamp, reason and the prior state.

## Safety boundary

Steel provides general fitness guidance, not medical advice. It must ask users to seek qualified clinical advice for symptoms, injuries or conditions outside ordinary training preferences.
